Off the clock

About

Most of what I'm interested in is older than I am.

I write software for a living and I like it — but if you asked me what I'd talk about for three hours without stopping, it would be a 426 Hemi, the financing of the Napoleonic wars, or why Raphael's School of Athens works as a piece of engineering as much as a painting.

There's a thread running through all of it, which is that I like things that were built properly by people who understood exactly what they were doing, and I like taking them apart to find out how. That's the same instinct that makes me build my own tools rather than pay for someone else's.

Interests

01

1960s muscle cars

The decade American manufacturers stopped being sensible. Big displacement, no electronics, nothing between the driver and the consequences. I can tell you more about a '69 Charger than is socially useful.

02

Military & financial history

These are the same subject and people keep filing them separately. Wars are won by whoever can still borrow money in year four. That's the part nobody puts in the documentary.

03

High Renaissance art

Michelangelo, Raphael, Leonardo — a narrow window where anatomy, geometry and patience all arrived at once. I look at these paintings the way other people read source code.

04

Greek mythology

The oldest surviving record of people trying to explain themselves to themselves. Every story is about someone who was warned and did it anyway, which has aged remarkably well.

05

Barbering

I cut hair freelance. It's the only thing I do where the feedback is immediate, physical and completely unambiguous — you cannot ship a fade and fix it in the next release.
